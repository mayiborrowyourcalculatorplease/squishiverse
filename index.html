Squishiverse

A place for a child to keep track of their squishy toys. Take a picture of each one, give it a name, rate how it squishes, and tag what it looks, feels, sounds and smells like.

Made for ages 9 to 11 · iPhone and iPad

Read the privacy policy

What it does with your child's pictures and words

  • The camera is the only way to add a picture. The app cannot open the photo library on the phone, and never asks to.
  • Faces are covered before a picture is saved. The phone looks for faces and paints a solid block over any it finds. This happens on the phone, with nothing uploaded to do it, and a solid block cannot be undone the way pixelation sometimes can.
  • The picture is shrunk and re-saved from scratch, at most 1600 pixels on its longest side. That drops what the camera attached to it: where it was taken, when, and on which phone. The original is not kept.
  • There is no account and no sign-up. The app never asks for a surname, a birthday, an email address or a location.
  • There is no server of ours. The collection is stored on the phone and, if the phone is signed in to iCloud, copied to your family's own iCloud account. Nothing in the app sends it to us.
  • The notes your child writes never leave the phone. They are kept in a separate place that is not connected to iCloud at all, so they cannot travel to a friend, or even to your child's own other devices.
  • No ads, no analytics, no crash reporting, no tracking, and no code from any company other than Apple.

Showing it to a friend

Kids want to show people, so the app can do that. Not on its own initiative, though, and not with anything typed.

  • Sharing is off when the app is installed. The invite button is not on the screen at all until a grown-up switches sharing on in Settings, past a question written out in words rather than digits.
  • Invitations go to named people, one at a time, through Apple's own sharing screen. A forwarded link does not let anyone else in. Nothing is searchable, browsable or public.
  • A friend can react with one of six symbols (❤️ 🥰 🤩 😮 😂 😎), point at one squishy to say "look at this one", or make a trade offer. That is the whole list.
  • There is no chat and no message box anywhere in the app. A friend has nothing to type into.
  • The only thing about your child that a friend sees is a nickname your child chooses, up to 20 characters. Everything else on the screen is about a toy.

Trading, if you want it

Trading is a separate switch, also off until a grown-up turns it on. A friend can offer up to three of their squishies for one of your child's that is marked as up for trade, and your child says yes or no. There is no money anywhere in the app, no prices, and no free text on an offer. Saying yes records an answer; any real swap happens between two families, in the real world.

Notifications

There are none unless somebody asks for them. Turning them on is behind the grown-up question; turning them off is not. When they are on, they arrive batched into one message and only ever report something a friend actually did. No streaks, no reminders, nothing built to pull a child back in.

Things we would rather you heard from us

Every app's safety page sounds the same. These are the parts that are not tidy:

  • Face detection finds most faces, not every face. A face in profile, partly hidden, very small, or in a reflection can be missed. The app says on screen when it has covered one, and it is worth a look at the picture before saving.
  • Turning the sharing switch back off does not take back access already given. It only stops new invitations. Removing someone who already has access is done in Apple's own sharing screen, and the app says so where the switch is.
  • The copy in your iCloud is yours to delete. Deleting the app clears the phone, including every note, but the synced part stays in your own iCloud account until you remove it there.
  • A grown-up can export the whole collection to a file, including the notes, which is exactly why that screen is behind the grown-up question. Where the file goes afterwards is up to whoever made it.

What a child actually does with it

  • Adds a squishy in five short steps. Only the name is required; every other step can be skipped.
  • Rates it on twelve things (overall, satisfying, squish, slow-rise, softness, stretchiness, texture, sound, smell, cuteness, tough and rarity), with half stars allowed.
  • Tags type, filling, colours, size, texture, sound, smell, and where it came from.
  • Picks a favourite, and a number one.
  • Keeps notes that are theirs alone.
  • Looks at the whole collection as a grid, searches it, and filters it.

Where the app is today

Squishiverse is not on the App Store, and there is nothing on this page to download. It was built for one family and runs on their own phones. This page exists so that anyone who is offered the app can read, first, what it does with a child's pictures and words.
